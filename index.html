The problem

An organizer's office answers the same questions all day, and the right answer keeps moving.

The same questions, many times a day

Close to an event, the office receives many messages a day. Most are variants of questions that already have a published answer.

Last month's answer can be wrong today

Fees, deadlines and regulations sit on the website and in PDFs and change as the event approaches. A reply that was correct last month can be wrong now.

Nobody knows everything

Staff work by department. A message that mixes topics gets partial answers from several people, days apart, and the person has to follow up.

The cost is staff hours, slow and uneven replies, and the risk of a confident wrong answer about money or dates.

How it works

It only knows what you have approved.

  1. Load and approve your sources

    The event website and your documents, each with a date. Your team owns what the agent knows and can see it at any time.

  2. People ask in plain language

    They get an answer with the source it came from. If no approved source holds the answer, it says so and points them to the right department.

  3. Your team sees what is missing

    Every question it could not answer is recorded, so the team knows what to add next.

Who it is for

For professional congress organizers, and for associations that run their own events.

The people who ask

Attendees and prospective attendees, speakers, sponsors and exhibitors. They ask on the event website or on a standalone page, without signing in.

The organizer's team

Staff register the sources, see what the agent knows, and work through the list of questions it could not answer. Each department is the contact for its own topics.

The first version

Public information only, and no personal data.

What it does

  • Answers from the organizer's public, approved information, with the source cited.
  • Says when it does not know, and names the department to contact.
  • Records every question it could not answer.

What it leaves out on purpose

  • Every asker is anonymous. Nobody is identified.
  • No personal data is used or disclosed.
  • Questions about a person's own record, such as their registration, go to the department that holds it.

Status
